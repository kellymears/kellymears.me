**Dual process theory** describes thinking as two modes. One is fast, automatic, effortless, and intuitive. The other is slow, deliberate, effortful, and logical. The psychologists Keith Stanovich and Richard West called them *System 1* and *System 2* in 2000, and Daniel Kahneman made the terms famous in *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011), which drew on decades of his work with Amos Tversky on heuristics and biases.

System 1 recognizes a face, reads a word, and drives a familiar road without conscious effort. System 2 multiplies 17 by 24, checks an argument, or follows unfamiliar directions. System 2 has limited capacity, drawing on [[Working Memory]] and [[Executive Function]], so most of life runs on System 1, and System 2 often just endorses whatever System 1 suggests.

The **Cognitive Reflection Test**, published by Shane Frederick in 2005, isolates the conflict:

- A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost?

The answer that springs to mind, 10 cents, is wrong. The correct answer is 5 cents. Many students at elite universities gave the intuitive answer, not because they couldn't do the math but because they never checked. Noticing that a check is needed is a job for [[Metacognition]].

The framework organizes many known biases as System 1 shortcuts left unchecked: the [[Anchoring Effect]], the [[Availability Heuristic]], the [[Base Rate Fallacy]], and [[Confirmation Bias]]. When System 2 is tired or busy, as under high [[Cognitive Load]], people lean more on intuition. Expertise, though, is System 1 trained well: a chess master's instant read of a board is intuition built from thousands of games, the same automaticity [[Habit Formation]] produces.

The model has real limits. Critics argue the two "systems" are a convenient sketch rather than separate brain structures, and that reasoning is better seen as a continuum. Some findings Kahneman's book relied on fared badly in the [[Replication Crisis]], notably social priming studies, and Kahneman acknowledged he had placed too much faith in them. The ego-depletion research behind [[Decision Fatigue]] has also proved hard to reproduce.

The vocabulary now also describes machines. A [[Large Language Model]] answering directly is often compared to System 1, and [[Chain-of-Thought Prompting]], which makes it write out intermediate steps, to System 2. Similarly, [[Automation Bias]] is a System 2 failure: accepting a machine's answer without checking it.
