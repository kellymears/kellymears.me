A **habit** is a behavior triggered automatically by a context cue, learned through repetition in that context. Habits differ from goals. A goal is deliberate: you decide to do something because you want the outcome. A habit runs without a decision. The cue appears, such as waking up, reaching the car, or sitting at the desk, and the behavior follows before deliberation starts. Studies of daily life suggest roughly 40% of what people do each day is performed in the same context almost every day, much of it on autopilot.

Habits form through a loop of **cue, routine, and reward**, repeated. Early on, the reward drives behavior, through the learning signal of [[Reward Prediction Error]]. With repetition, control shifts in the brain from goal-directed circuits toward the dorsolateral striatum, and the cue alone triggers the routine. Eventually the habit persists even when the reward is removed, which is why habits outlast the reasons they started.

The well-known claim that a habit takes 21 days has no research basis. A 2010 study by Phillippa Lally and colleagues tracked people forming new daily habits and found a median of about 66 days to reach automaticity, with an enormous range from 18 to 254 days, depending on the person and the behavior. Missing a single day did not derail the process. Consistency mattered more than perfection.

What follows in practice:

- **Context is the lever.** Habits are bound to their cues, so a change of context, such as moving house, starting a job, or traveling, disrupts old habits and opens a window for new ones. Researchers call this *habit discontinuity*.
- **Pre-decided cues help.** An [[Implementation Intention]] ("after I pour coffee, I'll take my vitamin") borrows an existing cue for a new routine.
- **Replace rather than erase.** An old habit's cue-response link fades slowly, so substituting a different routine for the same cue works better than resisting the urge.
- **Reduce friction.** Small changes to how easy something is shift behavior more than willpower does. A [[Defensive Default]] is the design version of this.

Habits save effort. Each automatic action is one decision fewer, protecting against [[Decision Fatigue]] and supporting weak [[Executive Function]]. The same machinery serves products built to capture attention: the [[Compulsion Loop]] and [[Variable-Ratio Reinforcement]] behind [[Doomscrolling]] are habit formation engineered for engagement.
