**Procrastination** is putting off something you intend to do, voluntarily, while expecting the delay to make things worse. That last part separates it from sensible prioritization or planned delay. About a fifth of adults describe themselves as chronic procrastinators, and Piers Steel's 2007 meta-analysis found it nearly universal in students.

The popular explanation, poor time management, has largely given way to an **emotion-regulation** account, developed by researchers such as Tim Pychyl and Fuschia Sirois. Tasks carry feelings: boredom, confusion, anxiety about failing, frustration, resentment. Avoiding the task gives instant relief from those feelings, and that relief is rewarding, so avoidance is reinforced each time. The cost lands on a future self the present self discounts heavily. Procrastination, in this view, is a short-term mood fix bought at long-term expense. That explains why it is worst for tasks that are unpleasant, ambiguous, or tied to self-worth, and why perfectionism and fear of judgment, as in [[Rejection Sensitivity]], drive so much of it.

**Temporal discounting** adds the economics. People value rewards less the further away they are, and the discount is steep in the near term, a pattern called *present bias*. A deadline three weeks away barely registers. The same deadline tomorrow is suddenly vivid. [[Time Blindness]] makes this sharper for people with ADHD, for whom "not now" can feel like "never" until the moment becomes "now."

Procrastination overlaps with but differs from other kinds of not-starting. [[Autistic Inertia]] can block wanted tasks without any aversion to them. [[Demand Avoidance]] responds to the demand itself. [[Executive Function]] difficulties make starting hard regardless of feelings.

What works follows from the mechanism:

- **Shrink the first step** so the starting feeling is small. The [[Pomodoro Technique]] makes commitment finite.
- **Pre-decide** with an [[Implementation Intention]] so the moment of choice is skipped.
- **Make the start social** with [[Body Doubling]] or a shared deadline.
- **Treat the feeling directly.** Self-forgiveness for past procrastination reduces future procrastination in studies, while self-criticism adds to the very feelings being avoided.

An unfinished task also keeps nagging, the [[Zeigarnik Effect]], which is why starting anything at all tends to make finishing easier. [[Parkinson's Law]] describes the other side: work expanding to fill the time is often procrastination given room.
