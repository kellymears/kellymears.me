The **Pomodoro Technique** is a time-management method developed by Francesco Cirillo as a university student in the late 1980s and named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used. The classic rules are simple: choose a task, set a timer for 25 minutes, work only on that task until it rings, take a 5-minute break, and after four intervals ("pomodoros") take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes. Interruptions are noted and deferred rather than acted on. Completed intervals are counted, making effort visible.

Its effectiveness has less to do with the number 25 than with what the structure provides:

- **A cheap start.** Committing to 25 minutes is far easier than committing to "finish the report". It shrinks the first step that [[Executive Function]] has to pay for.
- **Visible time.** A ticking timer externalizes time for people with [[Time Blindness]], who can't rely on an internal sense of how long they have been working.
- **A boundary.** A fixed box puts a deadline on every interval, which turns [[Parkinson's Law]] into an advantage, since the work expands only to the size of the box.
- **Protected focus.** Deferring interruptions to the break keeps one task in [[Working Memory]] and reduces [[Attention Residue]].
- **Built-in rest.** Regular breaks counter the tendency to grind through fatigue unnoticed, especially with weak [[Interoception]].

The common criticism concerns [[Flow]]. A timer that rings just as concentration arrives breaks exactly the state the method was meant to protect. Variants address this. *Flowtime* uses a timer to start and to track time, but lets work continue until a natural stopping point, then takes a proportionate break. For [[Hyperfocus]] the issue runs the other way: the timer is valuable precisely because it interrupts, and the break exists to eat and drink.

Pomodoro is a specific form of the broader practice of *timeboxing*, allotting a fixed time to a task and stopping when it ends, which is also used for meetings, spikes, and investigations so they don't drift into a [[Rabbit Hole]]. Shared online pomodoro sessions combine it with [[Body Doubling]].
