**Time blindness** is a difficulty perceiving how much time has passed and acting on events that lie in the future. The term is informal, popularized largely through Russell Barkley's work on ADHD, which treats the management of behavior across time as a core [[Executive Function]]. Most people carry a running internal sense of time and can feel next week's deadline as a pressure today. For someone with time blindness, time falls into two categories: **now**, and **not now**. Everything in "not now" feels about equally far away, until it suddenly becomes now.

The everyday consequences are recognizable:

- **Lateness that isn't disrespect.** Estimating how long getting ready takes, and noticing that the time to leave has arrived, both fail. The [[Fundamental Attribution Error]] reads this as not caring.
- **Deadline-driven work.** Motivation appears only when the deadline enters "now", producing the familiar all-nighter. It looks like [[Parkinson's Law]], but the mechanism is different: the work didn't expand to fill the time, the time wasn't felt until it was gone.
- **Lost hours.** In [[Hyperfocus]] the clock disappears entirely, and three hours pass like twenty minutes.
- **Steep discounting of the future.** Distant rewards and consequences carry little weight against immediate ones, a pattern measured in lab studies of delay discounting.

Everyone underestimates durations. [[Hofstadter's Law]] and the planning fallacy are universal. Time blindness is the same tendency at higher amplitude, combined with a weaker felt sense of time passing.

The remedies all **externalize time**, turning an internal sense that doesn't work into an external signal that does:

- Analog clocks and visual timers that show time *draining*, not just a number.
- Alarms for *transitions* ("start getting ready"), not just for the event.
- Breaking distant deadlines into near ones, so some piece of the work is always "now".
- Estimating from measured past durations rather than from feel. It is [[Fermi Estimation]] for one's own life, with [[Base Rate Fallacy|base rates]] instead of intuition.
