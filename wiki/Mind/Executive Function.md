**Executive function** is the set of mental processes that manage behavior rather than perform it: starting a task, holding the plan in mind, ignoring distractions, stopping when it is time, and switching when circumstances change. The standard decomposition (Adele Diamond's) names three core functions: **inhibitory control** (not acting on the first impulse), **[[Working Memory]]** (holding information while using it), and **cognitive flexibility** (changing approach or perspective). Planning, prioritizing, and organizing are built on those three.

The central insight is that executive function is **separate from knowledge and motivation**. Someone can know exactly what to do, want to do it, have time for it, and still not start. From the outside that looks like laziness or not caring, which is the [[Fundamental Attribution Error]] applied to a brain. Russell Barkley's account of ADHD puts it as a disorder of *performance*, not *knowledge*: the problem is "doing what you know, at the point of performance". This is why advice that adds knowledge (another explanation, another reminder of the stakes) so often fails, and why changes to the *environment* at the moment of action so often succeed.

Practical support follows directly from that model. Each technique moves an executive job out of the head and into the world:

- **Externalize memory**: checklists, visible task boards, notes kept where the work happens. A [[Zettelkasten]] is executive scaffolding as much as a filing system.
- **Externalize time**: visible timers and alarms, because [[Time Blindness]] makes internal clocks unreliable.
- **Shrink the first step** until it costs almost nothing to start. Task *initiation* is often the narrowest bottleneck.
- **Borrow structure** from other people, through deadlines, pairing, or [[Body Doubling]].
- **Reduce decisions**: defaults and routines spend no executive budget, while every open choice does ([[Hick's Law]] in daily life).

Executive capacity also varies within one person. Sleep, stress, illness, and [[Cognitive Load]] all drain it. A day of [[Masking]] or constant interruption leaves less for everything else, which is why the same task can be easy on Tuesday and impossible on Thursday.
