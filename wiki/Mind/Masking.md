**Masking**, or *camouflaging* in the research literature, is suppressing one's natural neurodivergent behavior and performing neurotypical behavior in its place in order to fit in or avoid harm. Examples include forcing eye contact, scripting conversations in advance, copying other people's gestures and phrasing, suppressing stimming, and hiding confusion or sensory distress. It is studied mostly in autism, where research led by Laura Hull in 2017 described three components: **masking** (hiding traits), **compensation** (working around differences with deliberate strategies), and **assimilation** (trying to blend in socially). The same behavior shows up in ADHD as hiding disorganization, over-preparing, and running constant self-monitoring.

Masking is rational. It is usually learned early in response to real social penalties, such as being bullied, excluded, or dismissed, and in the short term it works. The problem is cost:

- **It consumes executive resources.** Running a manual simulation of behavior that comes automatically to others draws on [[Executive Function]] and [[Working Memory]] throughout every interaction. That is why socializing can be exhausting even when it goes well.
- **It accumulates.** Long periods of heavy masking are associated with *autistic burnout*: a state of exhaustion, lost skills, and reduced tolerance for sensory input that can last months.
- **It hides need.** Successful masking is a reason many people, disproportionately women and girls, are diagnosed late or not at all. The support never arrives because the difficulty was never visible. This is [[Survivorship Bias]] in diagnosis: the profiles that pass go unseen.
- **It erodes identity.** People who have masked for decades describe not knowing which preferences are theirs and which are part of the performance.

The social side is the [[Neurodiversity]] paradigm's point in miniature. Masking is a response to an environment, and the most effective way to reduce it is to change the environment so that unmasked behavior isn't penalized. That means accepting stimming, not requiring eye contact, and stating expectations explicitly. Spaces where masking can drop are restorative for this reason. A [[Shibboleth]] marks who belongs. Masking is what people do when they've learned they can't afford to fail one.
