**Body doubling** is doing a task in the presence of another person who is not helping with it, simply being there, often doing their own work. The other person can be in the room, on a video call, or one of hundreds of strangers in a "study with me" stream. The term comes from ADHD communities, where it is one of the most commonly recommended strategies for getting started on tasks that otherwise won't start: paperwork, cleaning, the email that has been open for a week.

Formal research on body doubling specifically is thin; most of the evidence is practitioner and self-report. It sits on an old and well-established finding, though. **Social facilitation**, first documented in 1898 when Norman Triplett observed that cyclists rode faster alongside others than alone, and later refined by Robert Zajonc, holds that the presence of others raises [[Yerkes-Dodson Law|arousal]] and improves performance on simple or well-practiced tasks, while hurting performance on complex, unfamiliar ones. That matches what body-doubling users describe. It works best on tasks that are not hard, just hard to *start*.

Several mechanisms probably combine:

- **Borrowed structure.** The session has a start time and an end time that the person didn't have to generate internally, which supports [[Executive Function]] at its weakest point, initiation.
- **Gentle accountability.** Someone would notice if you stopped, even if they would never say so.
- **An anchor for attention.** Another person working nearby keeps the context "work time" active and makes drifting away more noticeable.
- **Shared time.** A scheduled session gives the hour edges, which helps with [[Time Blindness]].

The pattern has been productized: coworking sites, scheduled focus sessions with strangers, and livestreams of people silently studying. The streaming versions have a [[Parasocial Relationship|parasocial]] quality. The double never knows you're there, and the effect still works. That is evidence that the mechanism is the sense of being accompanied, more than any actual monitoring.

There is a parallel in software practice. Pair programming, and even [[Rubber Duck Debugging]], are partly body doubling: the second presence, real or inanimate, changes how a person works through a problem.
