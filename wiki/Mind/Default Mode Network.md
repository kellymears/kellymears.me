The **default mode network (DMN)** is a set of connected brain regions, centered on the medial prefrontal cortex, the posterior cingulate cortex, and parts of the parietal and temporal lobes, that become more active when a person is not focused on an external task. Marcus Raichle and colleagues named it in 2001 after noticing a puzzling pattern in brain scans: certain regions consistently *decreased* activity whenever subjects started a task, which implied they were busy during rest. The brain's "default" turned out to be an active state, not an idle one.

The DMN supports inward-directed thought: daydreaming, recalling personal memories, imagining the future, thinking about oneself, and thinking about other people's minds. Its typical product is **mind wandering**. A 2010 study by Matthew Killingsworth and Daniel Gilbert sampled thousands of people by phone at random moments and found minds wandering about 47% of the time. People also reported being less happy while wandering than while focused, whatever the activity.

Mind wandering has real value, though:

- **Incubation.** Stepping away from a hard problem lets it be reworked without directed effort, which is why solutions appear in the shower or on a walk. [[Rubber Duck Debugging]] and sleeping on a decision use related effects.
- **Planning.** Much wandering is about the future, rehearsing goals and conversations.
- **Consolidation.** Rest periods help integrate new memories, alongside [[Spaced Repetition]] and sleep.

The DMN generally works in opposition to the networks behind focused attention. When one rises, the other tends to fall. Deep [[Flow]] quiets self-referential thought, which is part of why it feels like losing oneself in a task. One hypothesis about ADHD holds that the DMN fails to quiet fully during tasks, so internal thoughts intrude on focus, which would fit the experience of attention pulled inward against one's will. [[Attention Residue]] is related: a previous task keeps running in the background of the next. Negative, repetitive wandering is *[[Rumination|rumination]]*, prominent in depression and in the replaying of social moments that drives [[Rejection Sensitivity]].

Boredom may partly be the experience of the DMN without anything good to work on, which is one reason a constant feed can feel calming and then draining. [[Doomscrolling]] fills every idle moment and leaves no room for the incubation that idle moments provide.
