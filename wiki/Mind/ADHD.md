**ADHD**, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, is a neurodevelopmental condition marked by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that start in childhood and affect daily life. Diagnostic manuals describe three presentations: predominantly inattentive (the older label ADD), predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, and combined. Estimates put it at around 5 percent of children and somewhat fewer adults, and twin studies find it among the most heritable psychiatric conditions, at roughly 70 to 80 percent.

The name is misleading. People with ADHD don't lack attention so much as struggle to direct it. The same person who can't start a dull form for three weeks can spend nine hours absorbed in something interesting, a state known as [[Hyperfocus]]. Psychologist Russell Barkley frames ADHD as a disorder of self-regulation and [[Executive Function]]: not a problem of knowing what to do, but of doing what you know, at the time it needs doing.

Common features include:

- **Time.** Difficulty sensing elapsed time and future deadlines, often called [[Time Blindness]]. The future feels abstract until it becomes the present.
- **Starting and switching.** Trouble beginning tasks without urgency or interest, and trouble stopping once engaged. This shows up as chronic [[Procrastination]] that has little to do with laziness.
- **Memory.** A smaller effective [[Working Memory]], so instructions, intentions, and open loops slip away.
- **Emotion.** Fast, intense emotional reactions and difficulty with [[Emotional Regulation]], including the painful sensitivity to criticism described as [[Rejection Sensitivity]].
- **Motivation.** Engagement tracks interest, novelty, challenge, and urgency more than importance. Dopamine signaling, discussed under [[Reward Prediction Error]], is implicated, though the "low dopamine" story is a simplification.

ADHD frequently co-occurs with autism, enough that the combination has its own informal name, AuDHD. The two can pull in opposite directions, with one wanting routine and the other novelty, and each can hide the other. Both sit within the broader idea of [[Neurodiversity]].

Treatment combines several approaches. Stimulant medications such as methylphenidate and amphetamines have some of the largest effect sizes in psychiatry, and non-stimulant options exist. Equally important are environmental strategies that supply externally what executive function struggles to supply internally:

- **External structure.** Visible timers, calendars, and checklists instead of memory.
- **Company.** Working alongside someone, [[Body Doubling]], makes starting easier.
- **Short intervals.** Time boxes such as the [[Pomodoro Technique]] create artificial urgency.
- **Pre-decided plans.** An [[Implementation Intention]] of the form "when X, I'll do Y" removes the decision at the moment of action.

Because it is invisible and inconsistent, ADHD is often mistaken for a character flaw. Many adults are diagnosed only after years of being told to try harder. Designing environments and tools that don't depend on perfect memory and attention, the aim of [[Inclusive Design]], helps everyone, but helps people with ADHD most.
