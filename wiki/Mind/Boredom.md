**Boredom** is the aversive state of wanting to engage with something satisfying and being unable to. A 2012 definition by John Eastwood and colleagues puts it that way: the problem is not a lack of things to do but a failure of attention to find a hold. It is restless, not relaxed, which separates it from rest or contentment.

Boredom is more uncomfortable than people expect. In a 2014 study, participants asked to sit alone with their thoughts for fifteen minutes were given the option to press a button that delivered a mild electric shock. Two-thirds of the men and a quarter of the women shocked themselves at least once, many after earlier saying they would pay to avoid it.

Several conditions produce it:

- **Too little challenge.** Under the [[Yerkes-Dodson Law]], low arousal means poor engagement. A task far below your skill sits at the opposite end from [[Flow]].
- **Too much challenge.** A lecture pitched far above you is also boring, because nothing can be grasped. Game designers tune a [[Difficulty Curve]] to stay between the two.
- **No meaning.** A task that feels pointless bores even when it is demanding, which ties boredom to the need for autonomy.
- **Repetition.** [[Habituation]] drains novelty from anything repeated, so the brain's interest signal fades.

Boredom is best read as a signal. Like pain, it says something is wrong and pushes you to change course. The philosopher Andreas Elpidorou argues that it works as a regulatory state, keeping people from lingering in situations that no longer serve them. It is closely tied to [[Curiosity]], the appetite that boredom frustrates, and to [[Reward Prediction Error]]: when nothing is better than expected, the dopamine system goes quiet.

The modern response is to answer the signal instantly. A phone fills every queue and pause, and [[Doomscrolling]] supplies a steady trickle of novelty that relieves boredom without satisfying it. Some researchers argue this costs something. Unstructured, slightly bored time lets the [[Default Mode Network]] wander, and mind-wandering is linked to planning and creative incubation.

Boredom is not evenly distributed. People with ADHD tend to be highly *boredom-prone*: their attention depends on interest more than importance, so low-stimulation tasks can feel physically intolerable, which feeds [[Procrastination]]. Under [[Monotropism]], attention pulled away from an interest can feel empty rather than restful. Boredom then sits closer to executive function trouble than to laziness.

Games have even built a genre around tolerating it. An [[Idle Game]] turns waiting into the core loop, rewarding you for coming back after doing nothing.
