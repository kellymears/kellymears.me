**Mindfulness** is the practice of paying attention, on purpose, to what is happening now: breath, bodily sensation, sound, thought, and emotion, and noticing them without immediately judging or reacting. It comes from Buddhist meditation, where it is one part of a larger ethical and spiritual path. Its secular form dates largely to 1979, when Jon Kabat-Zinn founded an eight-week Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) program at the University of Massachusetts Medical School for patients with chronic pain and illness.

A typical practice is simple to describe and hard to do. Rest attention on the breath. When the mind wanders, which it will within seconds, notice that it has and bring it back. The return, not the uninterrupted focus, is the exercise. Repeated, it trains several things at once: [[Metacognition]], by noticing where attention has gone; [[Interoception]], by attending to bodily signals; and [[Emotional Regulation]], by creating a gap between feeling and reaction. Brain-imaging studies associate practice with reduced activity in the [[Default Mode Network]], the system active during mind-wandering and rumination.

The best-supported clinical use is Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT), which roughly halves the risk of relapse for people with three or more previous episodes of depression. More broadly, a 2014 meta-analysis found moderate evidence that meditation programs reduce anxiety, depression, and pain, effects similar in size to other active treatments such as exercise or therapy rather than larger. Many popular claims about brain changes and productivity rest on small studies, a pattern familiar from the [[Replication Crisis]], and some effects in wellness apps may owe something to the [[Placebo Effect]].

It isn't harmless for everyone. A minority of meditators report adverse experiences such as anxiety, panic, or traumatic memories resurfacing, more often on intensive retreats. For some neurodivergent people, standard instructions misfire: body scans can be distressing for people with sensory sensitivities or poor interoceptive awareness, sitting still can be harder than the exercise itself, and walking meditation or focused movement such as [[Stimming]] may suit better. A common critique, sometimes called "McMindfulness," is that workplaces use it to make individuals absorb stress instead of fixing what causes it.

Mindfulness is also a counterweight to the attention economy. It trains the same capacity that [[Doomscrolling]] and [[Dark Pattern]] designs erode: noticing what has captured attention and choosing whether to stay.
