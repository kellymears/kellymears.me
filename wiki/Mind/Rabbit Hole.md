A **rabbit hole** is an exploration that keeps going deeper: one question leads to another, each answer opens two more doors, and hours later the starting point is out of sight. The phrase comes from Lewis Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865), where Alice follows a white rabbit down a hole into a world of escalating strangeness. Its modern sense, an absorbing and often unplanned descent into a topic, became common with the web, where every page is a set of links to more pages.

Hypertext is built for rabbit holes. A [[Wiki]] in particular turns every concept into a set of exits, and [[Backlink|backlinks]] add exits running the other way. The "wiki hole", starting at a recipe and ending at Byzantine succession disputes, is a familiar experience. Games have grown up around it, such as racing from one article to a target using only links. A well-linked [[Knowledge Graph]] or [[Zettelkasten]] is a rabbit hole by design: surprising adjacency is how such systems produce new ideas.

Rabbit holes are worth distinguishing from two neighbors:

- **[[Yak Shaving]]** is a chain of *prerequisites*. Each step is needed for the one before it, and the original goal is still the point. A rabbit hole is a chain of *[[Curiosity|curiosity]]*, and the original goal often stops mattering.
- **[[Doomscrolling]]** is a feed that delivers the next item without any choice. A rabbit hole involves active choosing at every step, which is why it feels like learning, and often is.

Rabbit holes interact strongly with attention style. Interest-led attention, as in [[Monotropism]] and [[Hyperfocus]], makes the descent effortless and the exit hard, and [[Time Blindness]] hides how long it lasted. The same chain that eats an afternoon also produces deep expertise, since many specialists describe their field as a rabbit hole that never ended.

Recommendation systems have industrialized a darker variant, where each suggestion is slightly more extreme than the last, a pattern studied in relation to radicalization and to the [[Filter Bubble]]. The structural difference from a self-directed rabbit hole is who chooses the next link, and what that party is optimizing for.

The practical skill is entering on purpose. A rabbit hole entered deliberately, with time set aside for it, is one of the best things the web offers. One fallen into an hour before a deadline is not.
