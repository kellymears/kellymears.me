A **rent strike** is a collective action in which tenants withhold rent to compel negotiation — over repairs, over increases, over conditions, or over policy. It works on the same logic as a labour strike: individually a tenant has almost no leverage and considerable exposure, while collectively they hold the landlord's entire revenue.

The threshold effect is the whole mechanism, and it is why organising campaigns are built around a **pledge**: participants commit conditionally, and the action begins only once a stated number have joined. That structure converts an individually reckless act into a collectively defensible one, and the announced target is doing organising work as much as arithmetic.

Legal exposure varies enormously by jurisdiction, and this is where campaigns are most careful. Some places recognise withholding for uninhabitable conditions, often requiring rent to be paid into escrow; others treat any non-payment as grounds for eviction regardless of cause. Campaigns therefore pair the ask with legal support, and public materials are typically reviewed before publication.

A **tenant union** is the durable form of the same organising: a standing association that can bargain, support members facing eviction, and act as a political bloc, rather than a single-issue campaign that dissolves after its demand is met or refused.
