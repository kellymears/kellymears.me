**Zettelkasten** — German for *slip box* — is a note-taking method associated with the sociologist Niklas Luhmann, who used a physical card index of tens of thousands of notes and credited it as a genuine thinking partner.

Its principles are few. **Atomicity**: one note, one idea, so a note can be linked to from many contexts without dragging irrelevant material with it. **Linking**: notes reference each other explicitly, and the links are the value. **Own words**: a note restates an idea rather than quoting it, because restating is where understanding happens. **Emergence**: no imposed hierarchy — structure appears from the accumulated links.

The last point is the one people find hardest and is the source of the method's leverage. A folder tree forces a decision about where a thing belongs, and any idea worth having belongs in several places. A graph refuses the question. See [[Knowledge Graph]].

The modern practice adds tooling — automatic [[Backlink]]s, graph views, full-text search — and generally relaxes the stricter rules. What survives is the core observation: a collection of small linked notes becomes more useful than the sum of its parts, and reviewing the links surfaces connections nobody planned.
