**Chekhov's Gun** is the convention that a story element introduced with conspicuous emphasis creates an obligation to use it before the story ends — canonically, a rifle hanging on a wall must eventually be fired.

The attribution is thinner than the aphorism suggests. Chekhov made the point in letters to fellow writers, in plainer words than the slogan: "one must not put a loaded rifle on the stage if no one is thinking of firing it", to Lazarev-Gruzinsky on 1 November 1889. The tidy "if in Act One" phrasing usually quoted is not a loose rendering of those letters but a separate line of transmission — third-party memoirs of Chekhov's spoken remarks, recorded by Gurliand in 1904 and Shchukin in 1911, and weaker evidence for that reason. What he offered was editing advice about economy — don't furnish a scene with props the plot never uses — not a law of narrative physics. Treating a compressed remark as a formal rule is a small case of [[Folk Etymology]]: a clean origin story outrunning the documented one.

Read as prescriptive, the principle instructs authors to fire every gun they hang. Read as descriptive, it names an audience habit: readers build expectations from emphasis and feel cheated when a foregrounded detail resolves into nothing. The second reading holds up better — respected fiction routinely hangs guns left deliberately unfired, as texture rather than promise, tolerated as long as it doesn't read as a commitment.

A red herring exploits the same convention in reverse, planting false emphasis to misdirect — which only works because the audience still trusts that emphasis usually means something. [[Fair-Play Mystery]] structures depend on that trust surviving the deception; overuse collapses it for everyone, the way [[Goodhart's Law]] describes a signal breaking once it is gamed. What a narrator bothers to describe at all is already a signal independent of what's said about it, a special case of [[Diegesis]], and an instance of the gap [[Plausible Mechanism]] names between what a detail implies and what it's shown to cause.
