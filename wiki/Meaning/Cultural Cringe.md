**Cultural cringe** is the internalized belief, held within a society, that its own art, media, accent, or institutions are inherently inferior to an external — usually former-colonial or metropolitan — standard, applied reflexively rather than as a judgment of actual quality. The term was coined in 1950 by Australian critic A. A. Phillips, who described local writers and audiences assuming a homegrown play or novel must be provincial simply for being homegrown, while the identical work would be taken seriously if it arrived with a London or New York imprint.

The mechanism is a colonial-era hierarchy that outlives the colonial relationship itself: a society's cultural institutions, education, and taste-making were built with an external center treated as the default reference point for what counts as sophisticated, and that reference point keeps operating as a habit of judgment long after the formal political relationship has ended. It shows up as a double standard applied by the very people it disadvantages — a local audience crediting a foreign accent with more authority in the same role, or a domestic critic reserving genuine enthusiasm for work that's been validated abroad first, what Phillips called the "Cringe Inverted" when it flips into an equally reflexive, compensatory over-praise of anything local specifically because it's local.

The concept generalizes past its original Australian and postcolonial contexts to any setting where a "provincial" identity gets measured against an unstated "cosmopolitan" default — a regional dialect judged against a national broadcast standard, a national film industry judged against Hollywood's budgets and gloss as the assumed baseline for what counts as competent filmmaking. The distinction worth holding onto is between recognizing a real, external quality benchmark and applying a hierarchy that isn't actually about quality at all, since the second is a self-perpetuating judgment that would attach to identical work regardless of its merit — closer to the mechanism behind the [[Halo Effect]] than to honest critical comparison.
