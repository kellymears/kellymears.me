**Doomscrolling** is the habit of continuing to scroll through distressing news, well past the point where any of it is new or actionable, driven by an anxious need to check rather than a desire to learn. The word surfaced around 2018 and spread widely in 2020, when a global event supplied both the anxiety and the unbroken feed of updates to scroll through.

The mechanism runs on two overlapping systems. One is negativity bias: threat-relevant information gets processed and remembered more readily than neutral or positive information, because attending to danger was, evolutionarily, worth more than attending to safety. The other is the feed's own design — infinite scroll and algorithmic ranking remove the natural stopping cues a printed newspaper or a fixed broadcast used to supply, so the behavior that satisfies the anxious itch (checking) is also the behavior with no built-in end. The result is a loop: anxiety prompts checking, checking supplies more anxiety-triggering content, which prompts more checking.

It's distinct from simply staying informed, which has a natural endpoint once the relevant facts are known; doomscrolling continues after that point, trading the appearance of vigilance for actual well-being, since most of what's consumed past the first update is repetition, speculation, or reaction rather than new information. It shares its underlying anxiety-driven-checking structure with compulsive email or stock-price refreshing, but the news-specific version is worth naming separately because the content itself supplies the emotional fuel, not just the checking ritual.
