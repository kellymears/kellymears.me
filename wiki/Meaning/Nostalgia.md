**Nostalgia** is a sentimental longing for a past period, place, or association, and — contrary to its original medical meaning — modern research treats it as a mostly positive, mood-boosting emotion rather than a pathology. The word was coined in 1688 by Swiss physician Johannes Hofer, combining Greek *nostos* (return home) and *algos* (pain), to describe a diagnosable, often fatal homesickness observed in soldiers and displaced people; only in the twentieth century did the meaning drift toward the wistful, bittersweet fondness the word carries today.

Psychologist Constantine Sedikides and collaborators have run the modern research program showing nostalgia functions as a self-regulatory resource rather than a symptom: recalling a cherished memory reliably increases feelings of social connectedness, self-continuity, and meaning in life, and people spontaneously reach for nostalgic memories more often when they're lonely, facing meaninglessness, or in physically cold environments — where some studies also report it raising subjects' perceived ambient warmth — though that finding sits in the embodied-cognition literature whose replication record is mixed, and is best held more lightly than the social-connectedness core. The emotion is functionally bittersweet by design: the same memory carries both the pleasure of the recollected connection and a mild sting that the moment is gone, and that mixture, not unadulterated pleasure, is what seems to do the psychological work.

Nostalgia is worth distinguishing from simple sentimentality about old things and from [[Cultural Cringe]]'s reflexive judgment of the present against a foreign standard: nostalgia's comparison runs against one's own past rather than against another group's present, and the past it idealizes is remembered rather than actually superior — a well-documented positivity bias in autobiographical memory means the nostalgic version of a period is reliably rosier than the period was when it was actually happening. Marketers and political movements both know this and exploit it deliberately, selling or campaigning on a remembered past whose warmth is partly a property of memory itself rather than of the historical record.
