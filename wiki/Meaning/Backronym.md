A **backronym** is an expansion invented after the fact for a string of letters that already existed — as a plain word, an initialism, or a name — reversing the usual order where the phrase comes first and the abbreviation follows. A 1983 word-contest entry coined *bacronym* for exactly this reversal, and the term settled into its more common spelling soon after. It belongs beside the [[Eggcorn]] and the [[Mondegreen]] in a small [[Taxonomy]] of retrospective reanalysis, though a backronym works on letters where the other two work on sound.

Genuine backronyms are usually deliberate and openly admitted. *SOS* was chosen as a Morse code distress signal for its simple, unmistakable pattern — three dots, three dashes, three dots — with no meaning attached; "Save Our Souls" was fitted to it only afterward, and stuck as a mnemonic anyway. Legislation and branding do this on purpose: an act named for a memorable expansion, a clinical mnemonic built to make a multi-part check easy to recall. Used this way, a backronym is a legitimate teaching and [[Naming]] device — the letters are scaffolding for memory, not a claim about origin.

The folk versions are the ones to distrust. *Posh* was never "port out, starboard home"; *golf* was never "gentlemen only, ladies forbidden"; *cop* was never "constable on patrol". None of these stories has any [[Provenance]] earlier than the twentieth century, long after each word was already current, and acronymic word formation itself was rare before then — a tell in itself. What the letters displace is not a mystery but something duller: *golf* is the Scots *gouf*, attested by the mid-fifteenth century and probably from Middle Dutch *colf* or *colve*, "stick, club"; *cop* is a shortening of *copper* (1846), an agent noun from the verb *cop*, "to seize" (1704). Only *posh* is genuinely of unknown origin. That is the usual [[Ground Truth]] — an ordinary etymology, occasionally an honest blank — overwritten by the same confident, unchecked claim [[Folk Etymology]] covers, sharing its [[Plausible Mechanism]]: a tidy story about letters beats a dull one about words.
