The **electromagnetic spectrum** is the full range of electromagnetic radiation, waves of linked electric and magnetic fields that travel through empty space at the speed of light. Radio, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays are all the same phenomenon. They differ only in **wavelength**, the distance between wave crests, and equivalently in frequency and energy per photon: shorter wavelength means higher frequency and more energetic photons.

From longest to shortest wavelength:

- **Radio** (meters to kilometers) carries broadcast, [[Wi-Fi]], Bluetooth, and cellular networks. Long waves bend around obstacles by diffraction and pass through walls.
- **Microwaves** (millimeters to centimeters) heat food by shaking water molecules; see [[Microwave Heating]]. Wi-Fi's 2.4 GHz band shares the same range, which is why a running microwave oven can disrupt it.
- **Infrared** is radiated by anything warm. People glow in it, which thermal cameras exploit, and most of the heat from the sun or a fire arrives this way; see [[Heat Transfer]]. Remote controls use it too.
- **Visible light**, about 380 to 750 nanometers, is a sliver of the whole. Our eyes evolved for it because it's where sunlight peaks and water is transparent.
- **Ultraviolet** has enough energy per photon to break chemical bonds. It causes sunburn, drives skin production of [[Vitamin D]], and makes some materials glow; see [[Fluorescence]].
- **X-rays and gamma rays** pass through soft tissue and can ionize atoms, knocking electrons free and damaging DNA.

That boundary matters for safety. Radiation above roughly ultraviolet is *ionizing*, able to break molecules directly. Radio, microwaves, and visible light are non-ionizing: no matter how intense, their individual photons lack the energy to break bonds, so their main biological effect is heating. That's the basis of exposure limits for phones; see [[Specific Absorption Rate]]. The photon picture also explains the [[Photovoltaic Effect]]: a solar cell responds to photons above a threshold energy, regardless of brightness.

Every hot object emits a spectrum set by its temperature, called blackbody radiation. The sun peaks in visible light, a stove element in red and infrared, a person in far infrared. This is why incandescent bulbs and candles are described by [[Color Temperature]], and why so much of an incandescent bulb's energy is wasted as heat.

Many optical effects depend on wavelength. [[Refraction]] bends short wavelengths more, so a prism spreads white light into a rainbow; see [[Dispersion]]. Small particles scatter blue light more than red, which colors the sky; see [[Rayleigh Scattering]]. Intensity falls off with distance by the [[Inverse-Square Law]] across the whole spectrum.

Human color perception compresses visible light into three channels, so very different spectra can look identical, the basis of [[Metamerism]]. Displays exploit this, producing every color from red, green, and blue alone.
