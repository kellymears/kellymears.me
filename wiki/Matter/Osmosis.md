**Osmosis** is the movement of water across a semipermeable membrane, one that lets water through but blocks dissolved substances, from the side with less dissolved material to the side with more. The water flows as if trying to equalize concentration. The pressure needed to stop that flow is the *osmotic pressure*, and it can be large. It is why a plant stands upright, through water-filled cells pushing against their walls, and why a wilted lettuce leaf crisps up again in cold water.

Living cells are bags of solution behind membranes, so osmosis governs them constantly. A red blood cell in pure water swells and bursts. In concentrated salt water it shrivels. Intravenous saline is matched to blood (*isotonic*) for that reason, and the kidneys spend much of their effort controlling the body's balance of water and salt.

Cooking uses osmosis and the related process of diffusion all the time:

- **Salting and curing.** Salt or sugar on the surface draws water out of food. This is how cured meats, gravlax, and preserved fruit are made, and why salted eggplant weeps. Removing water lowers *water activity*, the water available to microbes, which is the principle behind preservation by salt, sugar, and drying. It is also how [[Fermentation]] brines form from vegetables and salt alone.
- **Brining.** In meat, salt does more than move water. It diffuses inward and partly dissolves and loosens muscle proteins, a mild form of [[Protein Denaturation]], so the meat holds more of its own juice during cooking. *Dry brining*, salting hours or days ahead, draws moisture out first, then the dissolved salt is reabsorbed. Salt penetrates slowly, only millimeters per day, so time matters more than strength.
- **Maceration.** Sugar on cut fruit pulls out juice to make its own syrup.

Engineering runs the process backwards. **Reverse osmosis** applies pressure greater than the osmotic pressure to force water out of salty or contaminated water through a membrane, leaving the solutes behind. It is the main technology for desalination and many household water filters. The pressure required is a direct application of the hydraulics in [[Pascal's Principle]].

Sweating is osmosis's counterpart in [[Thermoregulation]], losing salt and water together, which is why endurance athletes replace both and why water alone can dilute blood sodium dangerously during very long events.
