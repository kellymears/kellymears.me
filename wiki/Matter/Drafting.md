**Drafting**, or slipstreaming, is moving close behind another object so as to travel through its wake: the pocket of low pressure and forward-moving air it leaves behind. Since [[Aerodynamic Drag]] dominates resistance at speed, the savings are large. A cyclist sitting a wheel's length behind another typically needs about 25 to 40% less power for the same speed. Deep inside a large peloton, computational and wind-tunnel studies led by Bert Blocken in 2018 put the drag on the best-sheltered riders at only about 5 to 10% of what a solo rider faces. That is less than many people's intuition, which pictures only the rider directly in front.

The rider in front gains slightly too. A follower fills part of the leader's low-pressure wake and reduces the pressure difference pulling backward on them, saving the leader a few percent. Racing cars and trucks show the same two-way effect, and NASCAR teams use it deliberately. Geese flying in a V save energy by a related but different mechanism: each bird rides the rising air from the wingtip vortex of the bird ahead.

Wind direction reshapes the formation. In a crosswind the sheltered spot is beside and behind, so riders fan out diagonally across the road in an **echelon**. When the road is not wide enough, the riders at the back are left without shelter, which is how races "split in the wind".

Drafting changes cycling from an individual time trial into a social dilemma. Riders share the work by rotating through the front, and whoever sits on the back without taking a turn, *wheel-sucking*, saves energy at everyone else's expense. A breakaway is a temporary cooperative of rivals who need each other to stay ahead of the peloton, which can draft its way back. That makes breakaways a small, fast case of [[Collective Action]], with free-riding, punishment ("attacking the sitter"), and unwritten codes. Similar dynamics appear wherever shared effort produces a shared good, including the [[Tragedy of the Commons]].

Drafting behind cars or trucks on public roads is dangerous, since the follower cannot see what is ahead and has no time to react.
