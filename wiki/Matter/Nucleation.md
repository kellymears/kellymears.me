**Nucleation** is the first step of a [[Phase Transition]]: the formation of a tiny seed of the new phase, a crystal, bubble, or droplet, from which the rest can grow. It explains why changes of state often do not happen at the temperature they should. Pure water in a smooth container can stay liquid well below 0 °C, and a soda can sit full of dissolved gas without fizzing, simply because nothing has started the change.

The obstacle is surface energy. A new phase forms an interface with the old one, and interfaces cost energy, much as [[Surface Tension]] makes a droplet pull itself round. For a very small seed, that surface cost outweighs the energy gained by changing phase, so the seed dissolves again. Only seeds larger than a *critical radius* keep growing. Random molecular motion must assemble one by chance, which in a clean, still liquid can take a very long time.

Nucleation comes in two kinds:

- **Homogeneous.** The seed forms in the bulk of a pure substance. This is rare and requires conditions far past the transition point. Very pure water droplets can be cooled to about −40 °C before freezing spontaneously.
- **Heterogeneous.** The seed forms on a surface, impurity, or particle, which lowers the energy barrier. Almost all real-world freezing, boiling, and condensation happens this way.

Heterogeneous nucleation is everywhere once you look:

- **Clouds and rain.** Water vapor condenses on dust, salt, and soot particles. Supercooled cloud droplets freeze onto ice-forming particles, and cloud seeding with silver iodide supplies extra ones.
- **Carbonation.** Champagne bubbles rise in streams from cellulose fibers left in the glass by drying towels, and some glasses are etched at the bottom to make bubbles on purpose. Dropping Mentos into diet cola provides millions of sites at once.
- **Boiling.** Water in a scratched pot boils smoothly from many points. Water heated in a smooth cup in a microwave can pass its [[Boiling Point]] and then erupt violently when disturbed, which is why boiling chips exist.
- **Hand warmers.** Reusable warmers hold a supersaturated sodium acetate solution that stays liquid at room temperature. Clicking a metal disc creates a seed, and the whole pack crystallizes, releasing [[Latent Heat]].

Controlling nucleation is central to cooking and manufacturing. Candy makers decide crystal size by when they stir: beating fudge as it cools creates many nuclei and a smooth texture, while undisturbed syrup grows few large crystals, as in rock candy. Ice cream is churned and frozen fast so ice crystals stay small.

The same pattern appears beyond physics. Many processes need a seed past some threshold before they become self-sustaining, from crystals to [[Fermentation]] started with a bit of an older batch.
