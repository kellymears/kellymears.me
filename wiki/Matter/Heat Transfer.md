**Heat transfer** is the movement of thermal energy from hotter places to colder ones, which [[Entropy]] guarantees will happen on its own. It happens in three distinct ways, and almost every practical problem in cooking, clothing, buildings, and electronics comes down to which of them dominates.

- **Conduction** passes energy through direct contact, as faster-vibrating atoms jostle their neighbors. Metals conduct well because their free electrons carry energy quickly. Wood, air, and foam conduct poorly, which is why they insulate. A steel railing and a wooden bench at the same temperature on a cold morning feel very different, because the steel pulls heat out of your hand far faster. Touch senses heat flow, not temperature.
- **Convection** carries heat with a moving fluid, air or water. *Natural convection* happens when warm fluid rises because it is less dense, as in a pot of simmering water or warm air rising from a radiator. *Forced convection* uses a fan or pump. A convection oven cooks faster than a still one at the same setting, and wind makes cold air feel colder, the *wind chill* effect, by stripping away the thin warm layer next to the skin.
- **Radiation** carries energy as infrared and visible light, and needs no medium at all, which is how the sun heats the earth across empty space. Every object radiates, and the amount climbs steeply with temperature, as the fourth power of absolute temperature. Glowing coals and a broiler cook mostly by radiation. A clear night sky lets the ground radiate heat into space, which is why frost forms on clear nights more than cloudy ones. The color of a hot object's glow is the basis of [[Color Temperature]].

Cooking is an exercise in combining them. A hot pan sears by conduction, driving the [[Maillard Reaction]] at the point of contact. An oven heats by convection and radiation. Heat then moves inward through food by conduction, slowly, which is why the center lags behind the surface and why [[Carryover Cooking]] continues after food leaves the heat. How much heat an object stores is a separate property, [[Specific Heat Capacity]], and a layer of vapor can block conduction almost completely, as the [[Leidenfrost Effect]] shows.

The body uses all three to manage temperature. Blood carries heat to the skin by convection, the skin radiates and conducts it away, and evaporating sweat removes far more through [[Latent Heat]]. [[Thermoregulation]] and [[Heat Acclimation]] are largely about controlling these routes.

Electronics fight the same battle. A chip conducts heat into a metal heat spreader, heat pipes carry it away, and fans push air across fins for convection. When that path can't keep up, the chip protects itself through [[Thermal Throttling]].
