**Instruction tuning** is fine-tuning a pretrained language model on examples of instructions paired with good responses. It's the step that turns a model which merely continues text into one that follows requests. Because it trains on fixed demonstrations, it's often called **supervised fine-tuning**, or SFT; see [[Fine-Tuning]].

A pretrained model has learned to predict the next token across a vast body of text; see [[Pretraining]]. Asked a question, it might answer it, or it might continue with more questions, as if completing a list from a quiz. Prompting tricks can coax useful behavior out of it, such as framing a request as the start of a document where an answer would naturally follow; see [[In-Context Learning]]. Instruction tuning makes that behavior the default.

The training data consists of prompts and target responses across many tasks: summarizing, translating, explaining, writing code, refusing harmful requests. Google's FLAN work in 2021 showed that tuning on many tasks phrased as instructions improved performance on tasks the model hadn't seen. OpenAI's InstructGPT, described in 2022, used human-written demonstrations as a first stage before further training on human preferences, a pipeline later chat models followed; see [[Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback]].

Writing demonstrations by hand is slow, so much instruction data is generated by other models; see [[Synthetic Data]]. Stanford's Alpaca, in 2023, fine-tuned an open model on about 52,000 examples produced by a stronger proprietary model, at very low cost. That's a form of distillation, and it raised questions about terms of service and about copying a model's flaws along with its strengths; see [[Knowledge Distillation]] and [[Open-Weight Model]].

Quality seems to matter more than quantity. Research in 2023 reported that a model tuned on about a thousand carefully chosen examples performed competitively, supporting the view that the model's knowledge comes from pretraining and instruction tuning mostly teaches it a format and a persona. Task diversity and consistent formatting also count for a great deal in practice.

Instruction tuning also defines the conversation structure. Training examples wrap system instructions, user turns, and assistant turns in special tokens, so the model learns where its own reply begins and which instructions take precedence; see [[System Prompt]] and [[Token]]. The same mechanism teaches models to call tools and produce structured formats; see [[Tool Use]].

The step has side effects. Tuning heavily on narrow data can erode abilities the base model had; see [[Catastrophic Forgetting]]. Demonstrations that always agree with the user, or always sound confident, teach the model to do the same; see [[Sycophancy]]. And because annotators write answers that look good, models can learn to imitate the style of expertise without its substance; see [[Hallucination]].
