A **side stitch** is a sharp or cramping pain on one side of the abdomen, usually just below the ribs, that comes on during exercise and fades soon after stopping. Researchers call it **exercise-related transient abdominal pain** (ETAP). It is very common: in surveys led by Darren Morton and Robin Callister, about 70% of runners reported a stitch in the previous year.

Its pattern gives clues to its cause:

- **Activity.** Stitches are most common in running, swimming, and horse riding, activities that repeatedly jolt or twist the torso. Cyclists, whose torsos stay still, get them far less.
- **Food and drink.** Eating or drinking shortly before exercise, especially large volumes or sugary, concentrated drinks, raises the risk.
- **Age.** Young people get them more often than older athletes, and the trained get them less often than beginners.
- **Shoulder pain.** Some people also feel a sharp pain at the tip of the shoulder.

Several explanations have been proposed:

- **Diaphragm cramp.** Long the popular explanation, the idea that the breathing muscle runs short of blood fits poorly: swimmers get stitches with little jolting, and cyclists breathe hard without them; see [[Diaphragmatic Breathing]].
- **Ligament stress.** The bouncing weight of the stomach and liver could tug on the ligaments that attach them to the diaphragm. That fits the jolting pattern but not the stitches in swimmers.
- **Irritated lining.** Morton and Callister's favored explanation is irritation of the parietal peritoneum, the sensitive membrane lining the abdominal wall, from friction with organs, especially when a full stomach presses on it. That lining is supplied by nerves that also serve the shoulder, which would explain the shoulder-tip pain; see [[Referred Pain]].

The pain itself is signaled by sensory nerves in the abdominal wall rather than by the organs; see [[Nociception]].

What helps:

- **Slow down** or walk until it passes.
- **Bend forward** at the waist, or push firmly into the painful spot.
- **Breathe deeply** and slowly, sometimes exhaling as the opposite foot lands; see [[Cadence]].
- **Tighten a belt** or brace the abdomen.

Prevention:

- **Wait two to three hours** after a large meal before hard exercise, and drink small amounts rather than gulps; see [[Fluid Balance]].
- **Build gradually.** Stitches are more common when training jumps; see [[Training Load]].
- **Core strength and posture.** Some studies link slumped posture to stitches, and strengthening the trunk may help; see [[Posture]] and [[Strength Training]].
- **Warm up**, so the pace builds gently; see [[Warm-Up]].

A stitch is harmless, but persistent or severe abdominal pain that doesn't ease with rest, or that comes with other symptoms, needs medical attention. Stitches are distinct from the involuntary contractions of a limb muscle; see [[Muscle Cramp]].
