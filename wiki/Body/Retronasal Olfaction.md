**Retronasal olfaction** is the perception of odour molecules that reach the olfactory epithelium from inside the mouth, travelling up through the nasopharynx, rather than from outside through the nostrils. The latter route is **orthonasal**. Both stimulate the same receptors, yet the route alone changes the percept substantially.

Taste proper is the small set of qualities transduced on the tongue — sweet, salty, sour, bitter, umami, arguably fat. Everything else in what is loosely called taste is retronasal smell, which is why food goes flat during a head cold and why pinching the nose while chewing removes most of a flavour. The brain refers the sensation to the mouth, so it feels like taste; that referral is a perceptual attribution, not anatomy. Chemesthesis is a third channel, carried by trigeminal fibres rather than chemical senses — the burn of capsaicin, the cool of menthol, the prickle of carbonation are [[Nociception]] and related somatosensation, not taste.

The route difference explains a familiar disappointment. Coffee grounds, cut grass, jasmine and petrichor are built from volatiles that behave well in air but not in the mouth: some never enter the retronasal stream in quantity, some are lost to chewing and saliva, some carry a mouth-side profile nothing like their headspace. Aroma vocabularies fail here too, since odour is poorly lexicalised in most languages — a standing example in [[Linguistic Relativity]] and a hard case for both [[Prototype Theory]] and any flavour [[Taxonomy]].

Regulation splits along the same seam: a compound may be approved as a fragrance yet not permitted as a food additive, because inhaled and ingested exposure differ in dose, route and clearance — the domain of [[Xenobiotic Metabolism]].
