**Body composition** describes what the body is made of rather than how much it weighs: fat mass, and lean mass, which covers muscle, bone, organs, and water. Two people with the same weight and height can have very different compositions, and composition predicts health and performance far better than weight alone.

The most common measure ignores composition entirely. **Body mass index**, weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared, was devised by the Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet in the 1830s to describe populations, not individuals. It's cheap and useful for large studies, but it misclassifies people with lots of muscle as overweight and misses people with little muscle and high fat, sometimes called normal-weight obesity. It also ignores where fat sits.

Location matters a great deal. **Subcutaneous fat**, under the skin, especially on the hips and thighs, is relatively benign. **Visceral fat**, packed around the abdominal organs, is metabolically active, releases inflammatory signals, and is strongly linked to [[Insulin Sensitivity]] problems, heart disease, and chronic [[Inflammation]]. Waist circumference, or the ratio of waist to height, captures it better than BMI does. Not all fat is storage; [[Brown fat]] burns energy to make heat.

Measuring composition directly involves trade-offs:

- **DEXA scans** use low-dose X-rays to estimate fat, lean, and bone mass by region, and also measure bone density; see [[Bone Remodeling]].
- **Bioelectrical impedance**, used by home scales, estimates water from electrical resistance and infers the rest. It shifts with hydration, so readings swing day to day; see [[Fluid Balance]].
- **Skinfold calipers** are cheap and consistent in practiced hands but measure only subcutaneous fat.

Lean mass matters most with age. Muscle is metabolically costly tissue that supports [[Basal Metabolic Rate]], glucose control, and independence later in life. Its loss is [[Sarcopenia]], and [[Strength Training]] with adequate protein is the main defense; see [[Muscle Hypertrophy]].

Daily weight is a noisy signal of composition. [[Glycogen]] and its bound water can swing weight by a kilogram or two, and salt intake shifts water further. Trends over weeks mean more than any single reading.

A familiar saying holds that muscle weighs more than fat. A kilogram of each weighs the same, but muscle is denser, about 1.06 grams per cubic centimeter against roughly 0.9 for fat, so a leaner body of the same weight takes up less space. People who begin lifting often lose inches while the scale barely moves.

In endurance sports, a low power-to-weight ratio can tempt athletes to chase leanness past what's healthy. Treating a body fat number as a target invites [[Goodhart's Law]]: the number improves while health and performance decline, as in [[Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport]]. Composition changes slowly, and [[Appetite Regulation]] defends against rapid fat loss.
