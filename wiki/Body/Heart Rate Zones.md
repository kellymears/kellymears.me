**Heart rate zones** divide exercise intensity into bands, usually five, defined by heart rate. They give a portable way to say "easy", "steady", or "hard" in numbers. Their usefulness depends almost entirely on what the bands are anchored to.

The most common anchor is also the weakest: an estimated maximum heart rate from **220 minus age**. That formula came from a 1971 line drawn through a handful of earlier studies, and individual maximums scatter 10 to 12 beats per minute either side of it, so for many people the "zones" are off by a full band. Better anchors, roughly in order:

- **Lactate threshold heart rate** from a field test or lab test, which ties zones to the physiology of the [[Lactate Threshold]].
- **Heart rate reserve** (the Karvonen method), which uses the range between resting and a measured maximum.
- **A measured maximum** from an all-out effort, which is better than a formula but still ignores where the thresholds sit within the range.

**Zone 2** has become shorthand for steady, conversational, below-LT1 riding or running, the base of [[Polarized Training]]. It feels almost too easy, which is the point. Its benefits come from the volume it makes possible without heavy fatigue.

Heart rate is an *output*, not an input, and it has quirks. It lags changes in effort by a minute or more, so it is useless for short intervals, where power or pace leads. It also drifts: over a long effort at constant power, heart rate creeps upward, especially in heat, as blood is diverted to the skin for [[Thermoregulation]] and plasma volume falls with dehydration. Caffeine, poor sleep, illness, and stress all raise it. A higher heart rate at the usual pace is a useful signal in itself, often the first sign of accumulated fatigue in [[Training Load]] terms.

Perceived exertion is the other half. Rating how hard an effort feels is surprisingly reliable for most people, and good athletes cross-check it against the numbers. When people with weak [[Interoception]] lose that cross-check, the zone numbers carry more of the load.
