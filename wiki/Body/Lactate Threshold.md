The **lactate threshold** is the exercise intensity above which lactate accumulates in the blood faster than the body can clear it. Below it, effort can be held for a long time. Above it, fatigue arrives on a clock measured in minutes. Most endurance performance comes down to how high this line sits.

Lactate itself has an undeserved reputation. It is not a waste product and it does not cause next-day muscle soreness, which is a separate process of micro-damage and [[Inflammation]]. Muscles produce lactate constantly, even at rest. It is a fuel: George Brooks' *lactate shuttle* work showed it moving between muscle fibers, the heart, and the liver, which burn it or rebuild it into glucose. The trouble above threshold is the accompanying accumulation of hydrogen ions and other metabolites, which interferes with muscle contraction. Lactate is a convenient marker for that state, not its cause.

Physiologists usually describe two turn points:

- **LT1**, the first rise in blood lactate above resting levels. Below it is genuinely easy, conversational effort. It is the top of "zone 2" in many [[Heart Rate Zones|zone schemes]].
- **LT2**, close to the *maximal lactate steady state*: the highest intensity where production and clearance still balance. Efforts just below it can be held for roughly an hour, which is the idea behind [[Functional Threshold Power]].

Training moves both lines. Untrained people hit LT2 at perhaps 60% of their [[VO2 Max]]. Well-trained endurance athletes sustain 80–90%. Much of that improvement comes from more mitochondria and better lactate clearance, adaptations driven mainly by large volumes of easy work, which is part of the case for [[Polarized Training]].

Measuring it properly means drawing blood from a fingertip or earlobe at progressively harder stages. Field tests, ventilatory thresholds (the point where breathing changes character), and power or heart rate tests all estimate the same boundary less directly. The threshold is less a sharp line than a steep bend in a curve, and different methods place it a little differently. Precise-looking numbers from any single method deserve some humility.
