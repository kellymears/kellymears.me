The **immune system** is the network of cells, tissues, and molecules that defends the body against infection and removes damaged cells. It works in two broad layers that cooperate.

**Innate immunity** is fast and generic. Skin and mucous membranes form barriers. Cells such as neutrophils and macrophages engulf invaders, recognizing broad patterns common to bacteria and viruses, and natural killer cells destroy infected cells. These responses start within minutes to hours and produce [[Inflammation]], with its heat, swelling, and redness, and, systemically, [[Fever]].

**Adaptive immunity** is slow and specific. B cells produce *antibodies*, proteins that bind one particular target, and T cells either coordinate the response or kill infected cells directly. The body holds a vast repertoire of these cells, each recognizing a different shape. When one meets its match, it multiplies into an army, a process called clonal selection, described by Frank Macfarlane Burnet in 1957. The first response to a new pathogen takes a week or more. Afterward, long-lived *memory* cells remain, so a second encounter is met quickly and often without illness.

**Vaccines** exploit that memory. Edward Jenner showed in 1796 that exposure to cowpox protected against smallpox. Modern vaccines present a harmless version or piece of a pathogen so the adaptive system learns it without the danger of infection.

The system needs balance. Too weak a response lets infection win; too strong or misdirected a response causes harm. Allergies are responses to harmless substances, and autoimmune diseases attack the body's own tissues. The *hygiene hypothesis*, refined as the "old friends" idea, suggests that reduced early exposure to microbes may leave the system poorly calibrated. The [[Gut Microbiome]] plays a large part in training immune tolerance.

Lifestyle shapes immune function:

- **Sleep.** Short sleep weakens immunity. In a 2015 study, people sleeping under six hours were about four times more likely to catch a cold after deliberate exposure to a cold virus. Sleep also strengthens the response to vaccines.
- **Stress.** Chronic stress raises cortisol, which suppresses some immune functions, a cost described under [[Allostatic Load]].
- **Energy.** Immune defense is metabolically costly, so underfueling, as in [[Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport]], brings frequent illness.

Exercise has a complicated reputation. Regular moderate exercise is associated with fewer infections and better vaccine responses. An older idea held that very hard efforts, such as marathons, opened a temporary "window" of suppressed immunity. More recent analyses argue that the immune cells were redistributing to tissues rather than disappearing, and that post-race infections owe more to crowds, travel, and poor sleep. Huge spikes in [[Training Load]] combined with poor recovery remain a reliable route to getting sick.

Like other adaptive systems, immunity benefits from manageable challenge, a parallel to [[Hormesis]].
