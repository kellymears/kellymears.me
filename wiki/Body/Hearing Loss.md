**Hearing loss** is a reduced ability to hear, and its most common preventable form is damage from noise. Hearing depends on the cochlea, a fluid-filled spiral in the inner ear lined with about 15,000 hair cells per ear. Sound waves travel along the basilar membrane, which vibrates at different places for different pitches, a kind of mechanical [[Resonance]], and the hair cells at each place convert that motion into nerve signals. In mammals, hair cells do not grow back. Damage accumulates.

Two main forms dominate:

- **Noise-induced hearing loss.** Loud sound overworks and kills hair cells, first those tuned around 4,000 Hz, which shows up as a notch on a hearing test. Single explosions can do it at once; years of loud work or music do it gradually.
- **Presbycusis.** Age-related loss, starting with the highest frequencies and spreading downward. Consonants like *s*, *f*, and *th* blur first, so speech becomes audible but not intelligible, especially in noisy rooms.

Exposure limits trade loudness against time. Decibels are logarithmic: every 3 dB doubles the sound energy. The US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health recommends at most 85 dBA for eight hours, and halves the allowed time for every 3 dB above that, so 100 dBA, typical of a loud concert, is safe for only about 15 minutes. Distance helps a lot, since intensity falls with the square of distance under the [[Inverse-Square Law]].

In 2009 Sharon Kujawa and Charles Liberman found that noise could destroy many of the synapses between hair cells and nerve fibers even when hearing thresholds fully recovered. This **hidden hearing loss** may explain people who pass hearing tests yet struggle to follow speech in crowds.

**Tinnitus**, a ringing or hissing with no outside source, affects roughly one in seven adults. It usually follows hearing loss. With less input from damaged regions, the brain turns up its internal gain, and the amplified background activity is heard as sound, a maladaptive form of [[Neuroplasticity]] much like phantom limb pain or [[Central Sensitization]]. Under [[Predictive Processing]], it is the brain filling in a missing signal. There is no cure, but therapies that build [[Habituation]] make it less intrusive.

The stakes reach beyond hearing. A 2020 Lancet Commission named midlife hearing loss the largest modifiable risk factor for dementia. One theory is that straining to decode degraded speech adds [[Cognitive Load]] and reduces social contact. Hearing aids, available over the counter in the US since 2022, are the main treatment, and in some studies they slowed cognitive decline in people at high risk.

The inner ear also houses the [[Vestibular System]], so some damage brings dizziness, and loud environments add to [[Sensory Overload]] for people already sensitive to sound.
