**Time as resource** describes a structure in which the constrained quantity is not money or health but the calendar. The *Persona* series is the canonical case: a fixed number of days, several activities available each evening, and no way to do them all. Nothing is expensive and nothing is locked — the cost of each choice is simply everything else that could have occupied the slot.

**This is opportunity cost made mechanical**, and it does something no resource economy quite manages. A currency can be re-earned, so a poor purchase is a delay; a spent evening is gone, so a poor choice is permanent. That permanence is what converts a menu of activities into a set of decisions, and it explains why these systems produce far more deliberation than their surface complexity would predict. The player is allocating against a hard ceiling in the same way a [[Token Budget]] or a [[Performance Budget]] forces prioritisation: the ceiling is doing the design work, not the options underneath it.

The structure also solves a narrative problem that resource economies cannot. Because the schedule is shared between the game's systems and its relationships, advancing a friendship and advancing combat capability compete for the same slot, and the player's stated priorities are revealed by their allocations rather than by dialogue selections. A game that asks who matters to you and a game that makes you spend Tuesday on them are making different claims, and only the second is difficult to answer dishonestly — one of the more effective structural answers to [[Ludonarrative Dissonance]] in commercial design.

**The characteristic failure is the optimal route.** Because the calendar is finite and its rewards enumerable, a schedule maximising total return exists and will be computed, published, and followed — after which the deliberation the design exists to produce is replaced by compliance with a spreadsheet, a case of [[Goodhart's Law]] executed by the community rather than the designer. Countermeasures are all forms of denying perfect information: outcomes that vary, opportunities that appear unannounced, or a ceiling generous enough that several routes are near-optimal. The tension is permanent, because the legibility that makes the pressure feel fair is what makes it solvable, and a guide that removes the [[Anchoring Effect]] of a first-playthrough guess removes the playthrough's shape with it.
