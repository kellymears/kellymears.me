A **multiplayer online battle arena**, or MOBA, is a team strategy game in which each player controls a single powerful character, a hero, and two teams compete to destroy each other's base. A typical match pits five against five on a fixed map with three lanes, defended by towers and filled with waves of computer-controlled soldiers that march toward the enemy on their own.

The genre came from players, not studios. A custom map for StarCraft called Aeon of Strife, made in 1998, put players in control of single units fighting alongside automated armies. Fans of Warcraft III built on the idea with Defense of the Ancients, or DotA, from 2003, which passed through several maintainers and became one of the most played custom maps in history; see [[Game Modding]] and [[Real-Time Strategy]]. Its success spawned commercial successors. Riot Games released League of Legends in 2009, free to play with paid cosmetics, and Valve hired the map's lead designer and released Dota 2 in 2013; see [[Free-to-Play]]. Honor of Kings, a mobile MOBA, became enormously popular in China.

Matches revolve around steady advantage. Killing enemy soldiers earns gold and experience, rewarding precise timing on the final blow, a skill called last-hitting. Gold buys items that strengthen the hero, and levels unlock abilities; see [[Experience Point]]. Areas between lanes hold neutral monsters, and teams fight over objectives that grant temporary power. Much of the map is hidden unless an ally can see it, so scouting and ambushes matter; see [[Fog of War]].

Early leads compound. A hero who gets ahead earns more gold, kills more easily, and gets further ahead, so matches can become lopsided long before they end. Designers counter this with bounties on leading players and comeback objectives, a balance between rewarding skill and keeping games contested; see [[Catch-Up Mechanic]].

Rosters of a hundred or more heroes make balance a continuous job. Each patch shifts which heroes and items are strongest, the community ranks them, and professional teams adapt their picks and bans; see [[Metagame]] and [[Tier List]]. Players are matched by hidden skill ratings; see [[Matchmaking Rating]].

MOBAs anchored the rise of esports. League of Legends' world championship has drawn audiences in the tens of millions, and Dota 2's annual tournament, The International, set prize-pool records funded largely by in-game purchases, with a share of sales going to the pot; see [[Esports]] and [[Battle Pass]].

The genre has a reputation for a steep learning curve and hostile communities. Losing feels like someone's fault in a five-person team, and matches last thirty minutes or more, so frustration builds; see [[Griefing]]. Later genres borrowed its structure, including auto battlers, which also began as a Dota 2 custom map; see [[Auto Battler]].
