**Disco Elysium** is a 2019 role-playing game by ZA/UM, a Tallinn collective founded by writer Robert Kurvitz, artist Aleksander Rostov and several others, in which a detective with total amnesia investigates a murder in the run-down city of Revachol. It has almost no combat; the game resolves through dialogue and [[Skill Check|skill checks]] against twenty-four skills — Logic, Empathy, Electrochemistry, Shivers and twenty others — split across four attributes. Each is voiced as a distinct interlocutor that interrupts, argues with, and sometimes misleads the player, a [[Diegetic Interface]] built from the detective's own psychology, so competence and psychology share one system instead of two. Revachol's crumbling blocks and faded murals carry as much history as any dialogue, [[Environmental Storytelling]] applied to a city.

**Checks resolve on two six-sided dice against a target set by skill level and modifiers**, layering bounded [[Nondeterminism]] over deterministic stat math, and the game distinguishes two kinds. White checks retry once the player raises the skill or clears a condition; red checks resolve once, permanently, and a failed one branches the story rather than dead-ending it — failure is authored content, not a reload prompt. Alongside this runs the Thought Cabinet: internalizing an idea — a conviction, a private theory, a joke taken too seriously — costs slots and time and reshapes stats once it locks in, making belief mechanical. The murder driving the plot functions as a [[MacGuffin]]: solving it matters less than what investigating it reveals, an inversion of what a [[Computer Role-Playing Game]] usually rewards.

The game is often cited for treating [[Ludonarrative Dissonance]] as solved, since its skills literally are its story; that reputation outlasted ZA/UM's original form — ownership passed through a disputed sale, and Kurvitz and several principal writers left in 2021. Disco Elysium remains a game built almost entirely from conversation, down to an [[Achievement Hunting|achievement]] list — 24 at release, 45 after the Final Cut — that mostly records what was said rather than what was defeated.
