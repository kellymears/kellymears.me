**Auto Battler** describes a genre in which the player assembles a roster during a shopping-and-positioning phase, then watches combat resolve autonomously once the round starts — no targeting, no timing, no input during the fight itself. The skill test is entirely in composition: which units to buy, which to bench, where to stand them, and when to commit gold to leveling up rather than rerolling the shop.

The genre traces to a *Dota 2* custom map, *Dota Auto Chess*, whose success spawned standalone competitors within months — *Teamfight Tactics* is the most durable of them, and it made the genre's real strategic layer explicit: an economy game (gold banked now compounds into interest later) layered under a synergy game (units of the same trait unlock a shared bonus at a threshold), so the draft is really two optimization problems running at once.

It's easy to lump auto battlers in with [[Tower Defense]] since both hand combat resolution to the system, but the boards aren't alike — a tower defense board is asymmetric with a fixed attacker path, while an auto battler's board is symmetric, and the strategy lives in unit synergy rather than lane geometry. It sits closer to [[Deckbuilder]] in spirit: both are about curating a pool (a deck, a roster) that plays itself out correctly once assembled, rather than about moment-to-moment execution.

The shop's randomized unit pool is a [[Variable-Ratio Reinforcement]] engine wearing a strategy game's clothes, and several entries in the genre have added an explicit [[Pity Timer]] on rare-unit odds — a tell that the designers know exactly how much of the genre's pull is a slot machine with extra steps, and have chosen to blunt the worst of it rather than lean in.
