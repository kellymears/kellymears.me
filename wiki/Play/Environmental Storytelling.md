**Environmental storytelling** conveys narrative through the arrangement of a space — objects, damage, lighting, the state of a room — rather than through dialogue or cutscenes. The technique came out of theme park design and was adapted for games; Henry Jenkins's 2004 essay "Game Design as Narrative Architecture" supplies the useful distinction between an *evocative space*, which borrows a genre's existing associations, and an *embedded narrative*, which the player reconstructs from evidence.

Its engine is the inference gap. A barricaded door, a mattress, a radio and one set of footprints leaving the room state nothing; the audience supplies the story, and it feels discovered rather than delivered. It is the [[Kuleshov Effect]] with objects in place of shots — meaning manufactured by juxtaposition, then attributed to the things juxtaposed rather than to the arrangement. That is also why the technique is cheap — no performance, no lip sync, and little to translate beyond incidental text, which sidesteps most of what [[Subtitling]] and localization cost a scripted scene.

Authored ambiguity is the deliberate version: place enough evidence to constrain a reading without closing it, then decline to confirm. Players routinely arrive at accounts nobody wrote, assembling coherent explanations from props placed for pacing, in the way [[Plausible Mechanism]] describes an explanation that fits and was never tested. This is a feature. There is no [[Ground Truth]] to a room, the audience's construction persuades better than any line of dialogue could, and unlike a cutscene it cannot be skipped.

Two limits. Legibility depends on placement and sightlines, so the discipline overlaps with [[Information Architecture]]: an audience that never looks at the tableau reads nothing. And a generated space cannot compose a tableau, which is why games built on [[Procedural Generation]] hand-place set pieces into generated layouts, and why [[Roguelike]] structures tend to carry their fiction in item descriptions instead. Asserting by implication also makes [[Ludonarrative Dissonance]] rare.
