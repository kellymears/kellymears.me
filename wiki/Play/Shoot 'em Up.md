A **shoot 'em up**, or **shmup**, is a game in which the player pilots a ship or character, shoots waves of enemies, and dodges their fire. It is one of the oldest arcade genres and has evolved into one of the most demanding.

*Space Invaders* (1978) started it: rows of aliens marching down the screen, a cannon sliding along the bottom, and enemies that sped up as their numbers fell, partly because the hardware drew fewer of them faster. It was a sensation in Japan and set off the arcade boom. *Galaga*, *Defender*, *Gradius*, and *R-Type* followed through the 1980s, adding scrolling stages, power-up systems, and huge bosses; see [[Parallax Scrolling]].

The genre comes in several forms:

- **Fixed shooters.** The screen doesn't scroll, as in *Space Invaders*.
- **Scrolling shooters.** The stage moves vertically or horizontally, carrying the player past scripted waves.
- **Twin-stick shooters.** One stick moves and the other aims, as in *Robotron: 2084* and *Geometry Wars*.

In the 1990s Japanese developers, especially Cave, pushed the genre into **bullet hell** (in Japanese, *danmaku*, "bullet curtain"). Enemies fire hundreds of slow projectiles in intricate geometric patterns that fill the screen. It looks impossible, but it is fair because of one design trick: the player's ship can only be hit in a tiny central point, often just a few pixels across, while enemy bullets have generous visible shapes; see [[Hitbox and Hurtbox]]. Players learn to thread through gaps that look closed.

The rest of the genre's design follows from arcade economics and pattern learning:

- **Patterns, not reflexes.** Stages are mostly scripted, so players improve by memorizing waves and planning routes; see [[Telegraphing]].
- **Scoring systems.** Points come from risky play, such as grazing bullets or chaining kills, so expert players play a different game from survivors; see [[Metagame]].
- **Bombs.** A limited screen-clearing attack, often with a moment of invulnerability, rescues the player from mistakes at a cost to score; see [[Invincibility Frames]].
- **One-credit clears.** Arcade games could be finished by feeding in coins; the real achievement is finishing on one, which is how the community measures skill; see [[Achievement Hunting]].

The *Touhou Project*, made by a single developer since 1996, became one of the most influential independent game series in Japan, with a huge fan culture of music and art.

Shmups faded from the mainstream as arcades declined, but they influenced later genres. The modern bullet heaven inverts the formula, with the player's weapons filling the screen and enemies swarming in; see [[Bullet Heaven]]. Shooting patterns have also become a staple of boss fights in action and rhythm games; see [[Rhythm Game]].
