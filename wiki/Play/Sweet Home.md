**Sweet Home** is a 1989 role-playing game Capcom released for the Famicom, designed by Tokuro Fujiwara as a tie-in to Kiyoshi Kurosawa's film of the same name — a haunted-mansion horror movie Fujiwara used as a starting point rather than a script to follow literally. Five characters carry one fixed tool each and cannot trade them, and the mansion's obstacles are cut to fit exactly those five: Kazuo's lighter burns away rope and cobwebs, Emi's key opens doors nothing else will, Akiko's medical kit heals, and a fresco hiding a clue must be photographed with Taro's camera after Asuka's vacuum clears the dust. Split the party wrongly and a room stays shut.

**Death in Sweet Home is permanent**, and the party never recovers a lost member — the game continues with fewer tools and options, closer to a structural [[Permadeath]] than a health bar. It stays completable anyway, because replacement items exist for the tools that die with their owners: a Match stands in for the Lighter, Pills for the medical kit. **Inventory is scarce by design**: each character has two item slots plus a separate weapon slot, and the fixed tool sits in one of the two, so choosing what to keep is a constant, consequential decision rather than a menu chore. **The mansion's history is told entirely through what is found in it** — diary pages, dying messages, a skeleton's implied story — a world functioning as its own [[Diegetic Interface]], the [[Environmental Storytelling]] a script would otherwise carry.

Capcom never released Sweet Home outside Japan, and no official [[Video Game Localization]] followed; for years it was known in the West mainly as trivia, reachable through [[Emulation]] and the [[Game Preservation]] work built around Famicom carts rather than a legitimate release. Its real export was structural. Fujiwara conceived and produced *Resident Evil* himself, handing direction to an initially reluctant Shinji Mikami and framing it as the horror game he had not been able to fit inside Sweet Home — a spiritual successor, not a remake. The mansion, the scarcity, the puzzle locks and the found-document narration carried over as the RPG mechanics gave way to real-time action. Sweet Home is the road [[Survival Horror]] did not take — the version of the genre that stayed a turn-based [[Computer Role-Playing Game]].
