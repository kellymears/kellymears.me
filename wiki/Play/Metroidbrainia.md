**Metroidbrainia** describes games that borrow the [[Metroidvania]]'s ability-gated structure and replace the ability with knowledge. The door was always openable — the player simply never knew the code, the shortcut, or which lever to pull first, not because a key was missing. *Outer Wilds*, *The Witness*, *Tunic* and *Animal Well* are the usual reference points, each building a world that looks static and turns out to be a puzzle assembled by re-reading it with new information. *Return of the Obra Dinn* is often added and just as often refused — the main survey of the category excludes it, on the grounds that its knowledge confirms answers rather than opening anywhere new.

The gate has consequences a mechanical unlock does not. **The first playthrough is unrepeatable** — the sequence of realizations cannot happen twice, since the second time the player already knows, and what remains afterward is closer to a [[Speedrun]] than a discovery. **External notebooks become part of the interface**: sketching a map or building the kind of deduction table that is really [[Constraint Propagation]] by hand is play, not bookkeeping, in a personal system closer to a [[Zettelkasten]] than a quest log — unlike a [[Diegetic Interface]], it sits outside the fiction entirely. And **spoilers do not diminish these games, they end them**: a stray answer, or content surfaced by [[Datamining]] before release, functions less like a spoiler than a cheat code, which is why [[Spoiler Etiquette]] around these titles runs unusually strict.

The term itself is fan-coined, first circulating in enthusiast criticism rather than trade taxonomy, and its boundaries are contested: reasonable people disagree about whether a given puzzle box belongs here or is simply a well-clued [[Fair-Play Mystery]] with better [[Environmental Storytelling]]. What holds the category together is the gate, not the genre it happens to be grafted onto.
