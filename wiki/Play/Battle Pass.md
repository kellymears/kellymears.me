A **battle pass** is a tiered track of rewards that players unlock by playing during a limited season, usually a few months long. A free track offers some rewards to everyone; buying the pass unlocks a premium track with more and better ones, mostly cosmetic items like outfits and emotes. When the season ends, unclaimed rewards are gone and a new pass begins.

The model was pioneered by *Dota 2*'s Compendium (2013) and became dominant after *Fortnite* adopted it in 2018. Its timing mattered: loot boxes, randomized paid rewards, were under attack from regulators in several countries as a form of gambling; see [[Gacha Monetization]]. A battle pass shows exactly what each tier contains, so it avoids the gambling comparison while keeping the steady revenue that [[Free-to-Play]] games depend on.

It works through several mechanisms:

- **Visible progress.** A bar of tiers stretching ahead, with the next reward always shown, pulls players forward; see [[Progress Indicator]].
- **Sunk investment.** Once bought, the pass feels wasted unless completed, so players keep playing to "get their money's worth"; see [[Sunk Cost Fallacy]].
- **Deadlines.** Rewards disappear when the season ends, which turns progress into an obligation with a timer; see [[Loss Aversion]].
- **Self-funding passes.** Many passes award enough premium currency to buy the next one, rewarding players who finish every season and punishing those who skip one.
- **Challenges.** Daily and weekly tasks give experience toward tiers, scheduling the player's sessions and pushing them toward modes and play styles they might not choose; see [[Compulsion Loop]].

For developers, the model aligns revenue with engagement and provides a regular schedule for new content. Each season brings a theme, new cosmetics, and often map or story changes, which keeps a [[Live Service Game]] feeling alive and gives lapsed players a reason to return.

The criticisms center on time rather than money. A pass requires dozens of hours per season to complete, so players feel they must play regularly whether they want to or not, and games start to feel like jobs. Players who follow several games with passes face competing schedules. Developers can tune the experience required per tier to push players toward paid tier skips. Critics count manufactured urgency and fear of missing out among [[Dark Pattern]]s; see [[Habit Formation]].

Variants have spread beyond shooters to almost every genre with ongoing content, and the model has influenced subscriptions outside games, where seasonal rewards keep subscribers engaged. Some developers have responded to the criticism with passes that never expire, letting players finish old ones at their own pace.

The term **season pass** originally meant something different: a one-time purchase of all planned expansions for a game. The two are often confused.
