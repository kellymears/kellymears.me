**Boss Rush** describes two related but distinct things wearing the same name. As a *mode*, it's an unlockable postgame remix — beat every boss the game has, one after another, usually with reduced or removed recovery between fights, as a victory-lap difficulty test for players who already finished the campaign. As a *game*, it's a title built from the ground up as sequential boss encounters with minimal connective tissue between them — *Cuphead*, *Furi*, and *Titan Souls* all treat the boss fight itself as the base unit of design, rather than as the occasional spike between long stretches of ordinary exploration and combat.

That second sense is the more interesting design claim: most games treat the boss as an exception to the normal rhythm, a spike that justifies the valleys around it. A boss-rush game removes the valleys and asks whether the peaks alone can sustain a full runtime — which only works if each individual fight is distinct enough in pattern and mechanic that back-to-back encounters don't blur into repetition, since there's no ordinary traversal left to provide contrast or rest.

This makes the mode a useful extreme case for thinking about [[Difficulty Curve]]: a boss rush is a difficulty curve with the recovery valleys surgically removed, all spike, and it reveals how much of a normal game's felt difficulty comes from *pacing* rather than from any single encounter's raw challenge. It also inverts [[Soulslike]] pacing specifically — where that lineage relies on a bonfire's guaranteed rest between attempts to make repeated failure survivable, a boss rush's whole premise is denying that rest and asking whether the player's skill, not the checkpoint structure, is what's actually carrying them.
