**Metagame** — "the meta" — is the layer of play that sits above a game's written rules: the prevailing set of strategies, character picks or deck archetypes that a competitive community currently treats as strong, distinct from what the rules formally permit. Two players following identical rules can be playing entirely different games if one has internalized the metagame and the other has not. A [[Creature Collector]]'s type-matchup chart is fixed by the rules, but which team compositions counter the field a given season is pure metagame, and it shifts with every balance patch the same way a fighting game's does.

The metagame moves for two reasons. A **patch or new release** changes the underlying numbers directly — a nerfed move's [[Frame Data]] falls out of the viable set, a new set in a collectible card game or a new season in a [[Battle Royale]] reshuffles what counters what, and this churn is deliberate policy in [[Live Service Game]] design, where the meta is treated as a knob to be turned on a schedule. **Community consensus** moves it without the rules changing at all, as players discover a stronger line and it propagates through guides faster than any single player could find it alone — the same discovery process that produces [[Datamining]] results and [[Speedrun]] route optimization, aimed at viability rather than clock time. This happens without opponents too: a [[Roguelike Deckbuilder]] community converges on which cards win runs, and that consensus is a metagame like any other.

Published **[[Tier List]]** rankings are where the metagame becomes visible and where it becomes **reflexive**: a list that ranks a strategy highly increases the number of players who adopt it, which increases the sample of wins attributed to it, which reinforces the ranking regardless of whether the original assessment was correct. The ranking stops describing the metagame and starts producing it — a direct case of [[Goodhart's Law]], where a measure built to track strength becomes a target that strength is optimized toward, and a tier list published early enough can entrench a metagame that a more patient analysis would never have produced.
