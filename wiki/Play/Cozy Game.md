A **cozy game** is a game designed to feel comforting rather than challenging. Typical activities are farming, decorating, crafting, fishing, and befriending a small community of neighbors. There's little or no combat, failure has few consequences, and the player sets the pace. The point is not to win but to spend pleasant, unhurried time somewhere safe and familiar, with nothing urgent waiting.

The genre has older roots. *Harvest Moon* (1996) turned farming into a game of daily routine and slow progress. *Animal Crossing* (2001) built a village where the clock runs in real time and seasons change with the calendar. *Stardew Valley* (2016), made by one developer, Eric Barone, inspired by Harvest Moon, became a huge commercial success and a template for many that followed. *Animal Crossing: New Horizons* launched in March 2020, as many countries entered lockdown, and became a shared social space for millions who couldn't see each other in person. "Cozy" as a label caught on around then.

Common design traits:

- **Low stakes.** No game over, or failure that costs little: a crop that dies can be replanted; see [[Permadeath]] for the opposite.
- **Gentle routines.** Daily cycles of small tasks: water crops, check the shop, talk to neighbors. Real-time clocks encourage short, regular visits.
- **Abundance.** Resources are plentiful; scarcity, the engine of tension in survival games, is mostly absent; see [[Survival Horror]].
- **Self-expression.** Decorating homes, designing clothes, arranging gardens; see [[Sandbox Game]].
- **Kindness.** Relationships built through gifts and conversation, in worlds where people are basically good.
- **Soft aesthetics.** Warm colors, rounded shapes, gentle music, and pleasing feedback when tasks complete; see [[Game Feel]].

Cozy games show that challenge isn't the only source of engagement. In terms of the [[MDA Framework]], they aim at expression, fellowship, and submission (play as a relaxing pastime) rather than challenge. Their appeal matches what psychologists say makes activities intrinsically rewarding: autonomy, a sense of competence, and connection; see [[Self-Determination Theory]].

The genre has critics and tensions. Daily routines can turn into obligations, and tasks gated by real-time clocks can feel like chores, borrowing techniques from engagement-driven design; see [[Compulsion Loop]]. Some note that farming games often present land and labor without the hard parts. And "cozy" has become a marketing label, attached to games that are anything but.

Related forms include [[Idle Game]] structures, management games such as the [[Colony Sim]] and [[City Builder]] played at a relaxed pace, and games that use [[Nostalgia]] for rural or small-town life as their emotional core.
