**Griefing** is deliberately spoiling other people's play for the griefer's own amusement, within or at the edge of a game's rules. The term took hold in online games of the late 1990s. A griefer isn't trying to win; the goal is the reaction.

Common forms:

- **Preying on newcomers**: killing or robbing players too weak to fight back.
- **Spawn camping**: waiting where players reappear and killing them before they can act.
- **Team killing** and sabotage: attacking or obstructing one's own side.
- **Blocking**: standing in doorways, trapping players, or destroying what others built.
- **Throwing**: deliberately losing a team match.
- **Harassment** in voice and text chat.

Griefers exploit the shared agreement that makes a game work, that everyone is playing by the same purpose inside a set-apart space; see [[Magic Circle]]. Richard Bartle's taxonomy of players includes a type motivated by imposing on others, and every open world attracts some of them; see [[Bartle Taxonomy]]. Anonymity and the absence of faces and tone reduce restraint, a pattern the psychologist John Suler called the online disinhibition effect.

The classic design case is *Ultima Online*. Unrestricted player killing in its early years drove away new players, and in 2000 the developers split the world in two: one copy where players couldn't attack each other and one where they could. Most players chose the safe copy.

Design responses:

- **Remove the tool.** Turning off friendly fire, letting players pass through teammates, and protecting fresh spawns take away the mechanisms griefing depends on.
- **Limit communication.** *Journey* (2012) paired strangers with no chat and no way to harm each other, and became known for unusually kind encounters.
- **Make it costly.** Reputation systems, vote kicks, and penalties tied to an account rather than a disposable character.
- **Match carefully.** Placing players by skill reduces strong players feasting on beginners, though experts creating new accounts to do exactly that is its own problem; see [[Matchmaking Rating]].
- **Report and review**, a workload that grows with the player base; see [[Anti-Cheat]].

Not every rough interaction is griefing. In games built around betrayal or open conflict, deceiving and robbing others is the point, as long as everyone signed up for it; see [[Social Deduction Game]] and [[Sandbox Game]]. *EVE Online* famously tolerates elaborate scams as part of its world.

Griefing thrives where stakes are high and losses permanent, as in survival games with [[Permadeath]]. It also follows the dynamics of any shared space: visible, unanswered misbehavior invites more; see [[Broken Windows Theory]]. Its history runs back to the first shared online worlds; see [[Multi-User Dungeon]].
