**Video game localization** is translation of a game's text and audio combined with cultural adaptation of what surrounds it — names, humor, iconography, age ratings — for a market the game wasn't originally written for.

Two problems recur across languages. **Honorific and title handling** loses precision fastest: Japanese address terms encode relationship and status with no equivalent slot in English, so a localization invents a substitute or drops the distinction, and languages with obligatory [[Grammatical Gender]] force choices the source text never had to make. **Compound terms** — a game's own coined vocabulary for a mechanic or item class — routinely translate as one lossy English word, a compound the source coined as a single idea rendered as an approximation.

**Source-language authorship** matters more than marketing suggests: a game written in Japanese and marketed in English has one canonical text and a [[Provenance]] the localized version derives from, not equals — lost once fan reference material treats every language version as equally authoritative, and complicated further by fan translations, the norm for a [[Visual Novel]] with no Western publisher, which circulate as a competing text of their own. **Dub and sub divergence** compounds this: a screen's [[Subtitling]] is translated for reading speed, a dub's line for lip flap and timing, so the two can say different things in one scene without either being wrong. Older localizations added a third layer: mismatched [[Character Encoding]] between Japanese and Western text systems corrupted names and menus, independent of translation quality.

The historical record includes outright alteration: religious and violent content changed or removed for regional ratings boards under [[Moral Panic]]-driven scrutiny, character ages and relationships rewritten to avoid controversy, entire subplots cut. Translation drift compounds over re-releases, producing conflicting terminology across fan [[Wiki]] entries and other community references — a slow [[Documentation Rot]] with no canonical fix, since each translation was accurate to its own moment.
