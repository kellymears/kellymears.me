**Visual Novel** is a narrative form told through static or lightly animated character portraits and blocks of text over a background image, where play consists mostly of reading and choosing between branches. The form's vocabulary is Japanese: Chunsoft's 1990s "sound novels" — *Otogirisō* (1992) and *Kamaitachi no Yoru* (1994) — set the template of text as the primary medium, where the "sound" meant music and stereo effects rather than voice acting. Both were unvoiced, and *Kamaitachi* drew its cast as featureless silhouettes instead of portraits. Two presentation modes are usually distinguished, only one of them descended from those novels: **NVL** format fills the screen with prose and lets the art recede behind it, closer to a printed page, and is the sound novel's direct heir; **ADV** format, a persistent text box across the screen's bottom with a portrait above it, predates them by roughly a decade and is already in place in *Portopia Renzoku Satsujin Jiken* (1983).

**Branching is managed through flags** — hidden variables set by choices and read later to gate content. A route can be locked until an earlier is cleared, and a true ending often needs flags from several playthroughs satisfied at once, the same [[Constraint Propagation]] problem a [[Metroidvania]]'s ability gates pose, worked in narrative variables instead of map edges. This is where the form parts ways with a [[Computer Role-Playing Game]]: its branches are authored and finite, not generated from a state the player can perturb freely.

**The genre's identity sits in UI and pacing, not simulation.** *Danganronpa*'s trial sequences and *Ace Attorney*'s cross-examinations dress a largely linear core in rhythm and text-as-spectacle rather than systemic depth, though both draw real structure from the [[Fair-Play Mystery]] tradition of clues shown before the reveal. Because text carries nearly the whole product, [[Video Game Localization]] and [[Spoiler Etiquette]] matter more here than in most genres, and completionists [[Datamining]] flag conditions for a true ending treat full-route [[Achievement Hunting]] as a puzzle in the game's own logic.
