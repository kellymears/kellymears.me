**Card drafting** is a game mechanic in which players take turns choosing from a shared pool, keeping what they pick and leaving the rest for others. Each pick serves two purposes. It improves the picker's own hand, and it takes that option away from everyone else. That double edge makes drafting one of the most reliable ways to generate [[Interesting Decisions]].

The best-known form is the **booster draft** in [[Collectible Card Game]] play, which spread through Magic: The Gathering tournaments by the mid-1990s. Players sit in a circle and each opens a sealed pack. Everyone takes one card and passes the rest to the left, then picks again from the pack that arrives, until the cards run out. A second pack is passed the other way, and a third back again. Each player then builds a deck from what they drafted. Because everyone starts from the same random packs, drafting levels the field between players with large collections and players with none.

Drafting tests more than card evaluation. The cards passed to a player carry information. If strong cards of one color keep arriving, the neighbor upstream probably isn't taking that color, a signal worth following; see [[Theory of Mind]]. **Hate drafting** means taking a card only to keep it from an opponent who would benefit. Experienced players weigh the strongest card in a vacuum against what fits the deck they are building, a judgment community pick orders and rankings try to capture; see [[Tier List]].

Board games adopted the pick-and-pass structure most visibly with 7 Wonders in 2010, in which all players choose simultaneously, so a game for seven takes little longer than a game for three; see [[Eurogame]] and [[Engine Building]]. Other games use an open draft from a shared market, where everyone can see what's available and the order of picks matters.

Video games use a single-player cousin. A [[Roguelike Deckbuilder]] typically offers one card out of three after each fight, so the deck is shaped by a long series of small drafts rather than built in advance; see [[Deckbuilder]]. An [[Auto Battler]] runs a shared pool of units that players buy from, so taking a unit makes it scarcer for others. Team games in the [[Multiplayer Online Battle Arena]] genre begin with a pick-and-ban phase in which teams alternately select characters and remove options from their opponents, a contest of its own shaped by the current [[Metagame]].

Sports leagues run drafts on the same principle, with the weakest teams picking first, a built-in [[Catch-Up Mechanic]]. Drafting also randomizes gently. The pool is random, but each choice within it is a decision, which suits players who want variety without leaving outcomes to the dice; see [[Input and Output Randomness]].
